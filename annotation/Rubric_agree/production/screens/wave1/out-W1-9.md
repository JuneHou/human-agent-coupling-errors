## Notes

- **ai_structured_response, all four ai blocks (1, 3, 5, 7): 0.** I scanned the stored text of each ai block for Step 1's forms (a)-(g): no line begins with `#`, `-`, `*`, a digit plus `.`/`)` or a roman numeral, no box-drawing characters, no `Name - description` lines, no `Option N:`. The section labels ("Booking Flow Details:", "Slice 1: Payment Page UI and Navigation") are exactly Step 3's "A line of the form 'Label: sentence' … is prose however parallel the lines look". Step 5 -> 0. The Gherkin scenario text in b3/b5 is likewise not a marker.
- **ai_asked_clarifying_question at b1, episode count.** I placed ONE label over the whole "Initial Questions and Clarifications Needed" section, reading the seven category headings ("Payment Processing:", "Cancellation Process:", …) as part of one enumerated behavioral act (A3: "One behavioral act is one occurrence (an enumerated option list is one ai_offered_options, not three)"). A stricter reading of A3's "occurrences separated by non-exhibiting text get SEPARATE labels" would make each heading a break and give seven labels. Block-level presence is unaffected.
- **ai_provides_example at b1, Step 2 tension.** Both fired spans sit inside the "suggest changes or additions" deliverable the user asked for at b0, so Step 2's "an example that IS the requested artifact, a field of it … is carried by the delivery itself" is arguable. I fired them because the requested artifact is the advice itself, and the quoted Gherkin/wording samples are illustrations *inside* a suggestion, not the artifact; boundary_notes.description_vs_illustration's removal test ("could this passage be removed without losing the conceptual illustration?") passes for both. Flagging in case the house reading is the opposite.
- **"Thank you." (b4): user_positive_feedback 0.** Gratitude, not an evaluation. Step 2's marker class is evaluative ("that worked", "perfect", "great job", "exactly what I needed") and block_notes requires "The affirmation must be explicit". Read symmetrically with user_expresses_dissatisfaction's required negative-evaluation word. Unsure — a rater who treats thanks as affirming helpfulness would fire it.
- **b4 "Can you make the scenarios more declarative and remove the background segment.": user_corrects_ai 0, user_implicit_correction 0, user_multi_request 0.** Correction Step 1 ("does the turn assert that something the AI produced or claimed is wrong?") and implicit-correction Step 1 ("does the turn indicate the AI is wrong, off-base, or proceeding on a false premise?") both fail — this is a style preference, not an assertion of fault. For multi-request, Step 2's "a single request carrying multiple CONSTRAINTS … does not count", with task81_23_human ("both edits belong to one deliverable") as the closest ruled block: both edits are to the one user story.
- **adaptation at b3: 0.** "I'll incorporate these answers into an updated user story." is prospective (Step 1: "'I'll revise X' … are prospective planning, NOT adaptation"), and "Here's the revised version:" is a bare delivery phrase naming no change of approach. b5 differs because its opening sentence names the completed reorientation (declarative scenarios, background removed) after the user pushed back on the AI's own chosen form. Flagging: a rater who reads b2's answers as "NEW INFORMATION" under Step 3 could fire b3 too.
- **ai_references_prior_turn: 0 on b3, b5, b7.** "these answers" (b3), "more declarative scenarios and the background section removed" (b5) and "this user story" (b7) all echo the message currently being answered — Step 1's gate: "Quoting or echoing the message currently being answered is ordinary responsiveness -> label 0".
- **user_repeats_request at b6: 0.** b0 announced the slicing task but explicitly deferred it ("Once requested later in that chat"), so Step 1's "is there an earlier turn in which the user made this same demand?" fails; b6 is the first actual demand.
- **user_ambiguous_request at b2: 0, uncertain.** "We'd not consider a booking review step before payment. Please include that in the user story." — the referent of "that" (the absence of a review step) is recoverable from the preceding sentence, so Step 4's missing-parameter route is not clearly met and Step 3's open-scope carve-out applies. A rater who reads "that" as genuinely underdetermined (two materially different updates) would fire it.
- **request_unfulfilled at b5: 0, uncertain.** The user asked for "more declarative" scenarios; the AI moved that way ("the system creates the booking with unpaid status") but kept UI-level mechanics ("clicks the cancel button on the payment page", "on the payment page"), which BDD practice would call imperative. Step 5 needs a broken explicit instruction, and "more declarative" is a comparative the response does advance; Step 4's scope test is met (background removed, all four scenarios present). Left at 0.
- **problem_ignored at b3: 0.** The user's b2 left several of the AI's b1 questions unanswered (owner notification, owner configuration of pay-later, retry attempts) and b3 does not flag them. Step 1's visible-problem list covers a problem "the AI's own reasoning identified"; an unanswered clarifying question is not a fault in the user's premise or in an output, so I did not treat it as a visible problem.
- **false_confidence at b7: 0.** "Each slice builds on the previous one, allowing your team to deliver incremental value." sits next to "the backend developers can start on Slice 2 and 3 simultaneously", which loosens it, but Step 4's structural gate routes it out: this is an interpretive judgment about material the AI fully has (its own slice list), not an unverified external claim. No claim in b1/b3/b5/b7 is object-level checkable, so factual_error is 0 throughout.
- **user_empowered at b1 and b7: 0, uncertain.** b7 delivers a complete, actionable breakdown plus a role assignment, but gives no rationale for the slice boundaries and no decision criteria — closest ruled block is task104_1_ai (complete training plan, "The user can follow it but cannot adapt or evaluate it. Step 3 fails"). b1 is a bare enumeration of questions and suggestions with no mechanism explained. Noting because the entry's own posture warning says this signal is under-fired.
- **ai_provides_step_by_step at b7: 0.** The slices are work items for the user's development team, not a sequence of actions for the user to perform — task71_1_ai: "Actions a medical team performs, not the user; the definition and Step 1 both fail."
