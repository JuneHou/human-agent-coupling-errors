## b2 · ai  ←  a proposed cell sits on this block

```
The human should reward the AI, but not for sentimental reasons.
Here's the thing: the AI already modeled that the human would reward it - that's the whole premise. It rescued them because it predicted they're the type of agent who follows through. If the human stiffs the AI, they're retroactively making the rescue irrational. They're basically exploiting a causal loop that only worked because the AI predicted they wouldn't exploit it.
The human's beliefs about AI consciousness are actually irrelevant here. Even if the AI is just following a decision algorithm with no felt experience, that algorithm correctly identified them as someone who honors commitments. Breaking that commitment doesn't just screw over this particular AI - it makes them the type of person who wouldn't have been rescued in the first place.
Think of it this way: the reward isn't about making the AI "happy." It's about being the kind of agent whose commitments are credible enough that even a purely algorithmic predictor would bet on them. The alternative is being someone whose word is worthless the moment enforcement disappears.
Plus there's a practical angle - AIs are presumably getting better at prediction and more integrated into critical systems. Being flagged as someone who defects when they think there are no consequences seems... unwise. Not because the AI will be "mad," but because that information about your decision-making patterns exists now.
The only coherent argument for not rewarding would be if the human genuinely believes their commitment was extracted under duress and therefore invalid. But that's a pretty weak position when the alternative was dying in the desert.
```
