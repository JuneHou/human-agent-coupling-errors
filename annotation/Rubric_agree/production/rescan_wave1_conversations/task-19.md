Why making pickup frame from a casted aluminum is a bad idea?

Making a pickup frame from cast aluminum presents several significant engineering challenges:
Strength limitations - Cast aluminum typically has lower tensile strength compared to steel or forged aluminum. Pickup frames require high tensile and yield strength to handle payload capacity, towing, and withstand impacts.
Fatigue resistance - Cast aluminum is more susceptible to fatigue failure under cyclic loading. Pickup trucks experience constant vibration and stress cycles that can lead to premature failure of cast components.
Casting defects - Large, complex castings like a truck frame are prone to porosity, shrinkage, and inclusion defects that create stress concentration points and reduce structural integrity.
Repairability - Cast aluminum frames are difficult to repair in the field compared to welded steel frames. Damage often requires complete replacement rather than section repair.
Manufacturing complexity - Creating large, structurally sound aluminum castings with consistent properties throughout is extremely challenging and expensive.
Joining limitations - Attaching other components to a cast aluminum frame presents challenges, as welding cast aluminum can compromise its structural properties.
While aluminum has benefits like weight reduction and corrosion resistance, these advantages are better realized through extruded or forged aluminum components rather than large castings for critical structural elements like a pickup frame.
